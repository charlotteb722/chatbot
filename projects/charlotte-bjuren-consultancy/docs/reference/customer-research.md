# Customer research

Research date: 26 November 2026. Method: web search (results summarised by the search tool). Direct page fetching was blocked for some hosts in this environment, so most sources were read as search summaries, not opened in full. Treat directory pricing as indicative.

**Limitation:** no Reddit threads matched the searches run. Customer language below is drawn from Charlotte's own brief, UK business forums and UK provider guides. A second pass with Reddit (r/smallbusinessuk, r/UKPersonalFinance) and Google reviews of local web designers is recommended once network access allows it.

## Customer profiles

These are working profiles built from the brief. They are not based on interviews. Validating them with three or four real conversations would be the single most useful research step.

### 1. The local trade (joiner, electrician, plumber, landscaper)
- Gets most work by word of mouth and Facebook. Busy on site, does admin in the evening.
- Wants the phone to ring with the right kind of job in the right area.
- Worries: paying for something that does nothing; monthly fees that creep up; not being able to change anything themselves.
- Needs from the site: services, area covered, photos of real work, reviews, a phone number that works with one tap.

### 2. The coach, consultant or independent professional
- Often a career-changer or recently self-employed. Credibility matters more than volume.
- Wants enquiries from people who already understand the offer and the price range.
- Worries: looking amateur; generic template sites; technical jargon.
- Needs: clear explanation of who they help, a booking link, an About page that reads like a person.

### 3. Café, hospitality, tourism or accommodation
- Seasonal trade, visitors searching on phones, often relying on Instagram and booking platforms.
- Wants: opening hours, menu, location, and bookings without paying high commission.
- Worries: keeping hours and menus up to date; another login to manage.
- Needs: fast mobile pages, map and directions, simple way to update hours and menus.

### 4. The start-up or side-business owner
- Very price-sensitive, often has a logo from Canva and an Instagram page.
- Wants to look established quickly and cheaply.
- Worries: being sold more than they need.
- Needs: a small, honest package and clear explanation of what they will pay each year.

## Customer search behaviour (qualitative)

No search volume data was available, and none is claimed. Likely search patterns, to test with Google Search Console after launch:

- Local intent: "web designer Crieff", "website design Perthshire", "web design Perth", "website for my business Perthshire"
- Problem intent: "how much does a website cost Scotland", "who owns my domain", "move website from Wix"
- AI intent: "AI training for small business Scotland", "ChatGPT help for small business"
- Industry intent: "website for tradesmen", "café website", "coach website"

Directory and lead-generation pages (Bark, BizHub365, Yell) rank for many local terms. A clear, honest local site with a Google Business Profile has a realistic chance in the Crieff and Strathearn area, where there appear to be few dedicated providers. Perth city terms will be more competitive.

## Implications for the website

1. Make ownership a headline promise: the client owns the domain, the accounts and the code. This answers the most consistent fear found.
2. Publish starting prices and explain yearly running costs in pounds. Uncertainty about cost is the clearest gap in local competitors' sites found so far.
3. No long contracts on maintenance. Monthly, cancel with notice.
4. Plain English throughout, with a short explainer on hosting and domains.
5. AI coaching should start from one real task in the customer's business, not from the technology.
6. Mention free public support (Business Gateway) honestly where relevant. It builds trust and costs nothing.
