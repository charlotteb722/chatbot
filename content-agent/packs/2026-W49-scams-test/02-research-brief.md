# Research brief: AI is making scam emails more convincing

Week 2026-W49-scams-test · Researched 22 November 2026 · Topic chosen by Charlotte · Platform overrides: none

APPROVED (automatic verification gate, 22 November 2026)

---

## Summary

The ICO warns that criminals are using AI to make attacks faster and harder to spot, from convincing phishing emails that impersonate colleagues, clients or suppliers, to cloned voices and deepfake video. Its advice for organisations includes multi-factor authentication on email, regular staff training on AI-powered tricks, and an incident plan with contact details kept offline. GOV.UK gives small businesses free, practical help: staff training that takes under 30 minutes, a Cyber Action Toolkit for sole traders and small businesses, free 30-minute sessions with a Cyber Advisor, and a simple reporting route for suspicious emails and texts.

## Assumptions and interpretation

- **A1.** A "pause, check, report" routine is a fair, simple summary of F2, F3, F10 and F11 for small teams. It's advice.
- **A2.** Checking any change to bank details or any urgent payment request by phoning a number you already have (not one in the email) is sensible practice that follows from F2 and F3. It's my advice, not an ICO or GOV.UK quote, and must be framed that way.
- **A3.** Agreeing a simple "check word" or call-back rule within a small team for urgent requests by phone or video follows from F3. It's advice.
- **A4.** No statistics in the posts. No claims that any single step stops all scams.
