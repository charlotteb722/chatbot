## Summary

Microsoft says Copilot Chat, a web-grounded AI assistant, is included at no additional cost with eligible Microsoft 365 plans, and that anyone without a paid Copilot licence can try it by signing in with their work account. It works mainly from the web. It uses your own documents only when you paste, upload or open them. Signing in with a work account gives it enterprise data protection. The paid Microsoft 365 Copilot Business add-on unlocks Copilot inside Teams, Word, Excel and PowerPoint, plus Microsoft's research and analysis agents, and Microsoft is promoting it to small businesses until 31 December 2026. The practical message: try the assistant you already have on a real task first, then decide whether the add-on would earn its keep.

## Audience

UK sole traders and small businesses using a **Microsoft 365 business plan with a work account** (for example a business email address set up through Microsoft 365). The content isn't for people using only a personal Microsoft account. Microsoft's wording refers to work accounts (F2, F10).

## Assumptions and interpretation

- **A1.** A UK small business on an eligible Microsoft 365 business plan can probably use Copilot Chat today at no extra cost (F1, F2, F5). The UK pricing page in pounds suggests the UK isn't among the markets where Copilot is unavailable (F15). But Microsoft doesn't name which plans are "eligible" for Copilot Chat on the pages read, so the posts should say "eligible Microsoft 365 plans" and suggest signing in with a work account to check.
- **A2.** The "three things to try this week" should use only what Copilot Chat is documented to do: summarise a web page in Edge (F7), work with a document you upload or paste (F8), and help with the email you have open in Outlook (F6, F8). These are suggestions, not Microsoft claims about results.
- **A3.** A simple "is the add-on worth it?" test (opinion): list the weekly tasks you do inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint or Teams that the add-on would cover (F3, F9). If there are few, Copilot Chat may be enough for now.
- **A4.** Enterprise data protection (F10) is reassuring, but small businesses should still avoid pasting more personal data than a task needs. This is good-practice advice, not a Microsoft statement.
- **A5.** No claims about time saved or business results.
- **A6.** Avoid sounding like a Microsoft advert. Present the add-on neutrally, as an option to test, not a recommendation to buy.

## Suggested practical takeaway

This week, sign in to Copilot Chat with your work account (F2). Try three real tasks:
1. Summarise a supplier's or competitor's web page in Edge (F7).
2. Upload a document you'd normally read in full and ask for the key points (F8).
3. With a customer email open in Outlook, ask Copilot Chat to draft a reply (F6, F8).

Check everything before using it. Then list the Word, Excel and Teams tasks you'd want help with. That list tells you whether the add-on is worth trialling (F3, F9, A3).
