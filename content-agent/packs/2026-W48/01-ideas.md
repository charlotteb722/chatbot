## 1. Customers are using AI to write data requests. What the ICO says small businesses must do

- **What's happening:** on 11 November 2026 the ICO published new guidance on subject access requests (SARs) written with AI. A request "isn't invalid just because a person used AI to help draft it", and you must treat it like any other SAR. AI-written requests can be long, broad, or quote the wrong laws.
- **Who it helps:** any UK sole trader or small business that holds customer, client or staff information. That's almost all of them, especially businesses with no dedicated data protection person.
- **Why it matters:** these requests can look confusing or even fake, and it's tempting to ignore them. The ICO is clear that you can't. It also confirms you can ask for clarification, and that you only need a reasonable and proportionate search.
- **Why now:** the guidance is 11 days old, and AI tools make SARs easier for anyone to send.
- **Angle:** "Got a long, odd-looking data request? It probably came from AI, and it still counts." A short checklist: recognise it, log the date, clarify if unclear, search proportionately, reply on time. It shows the responsible side of AI, which builds trust.
- **Best platform:** LinkedIn (professional), carousel (checklist), blog
- **Type:** news plus how-to
- **Lead source (opened 22 November 2026):**
  - [ICO: Subject access requests generated by artificial intelligence](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/11/subject-access-requests-generated-by-artificial-intelligence/), published 11 November 2026
- **Check in research:** the response deadline wording, the "complex" extension rules, and what the ICO says about manifestly unfounded or excessive requests.

## 2. Already paying for Microsoft 365? You may already have an AI assistant

- **What's happening:** Microsoft says "Copilot Chat is available to Microsoft business users", and that what you can do depends on whether you also have a paid Copilot add-on licence. Microsoft is promoting Copilot Business to small firms with trial offers until 31 December 2026.
- **Who it helps:** small businesses and sole traders on Microsoft 365 business plans who haven't tried AI yet, or who are weighing up whether to pay for the add-on.
- **Why it matters:** many people pay for an add-on without trying what they already have. Knowing the difference between Copilot Chat and the paid add-on helps them make a sensible decision.
- **Why now:** Microsoft's year-end trial push (Copilot in 30, until 31 December 2026) means small firms are being offered the paid version now.
- **Angle:** "Try what you already have first." Three useful things to do in Copilot Chat this week, then a simple test for whether the paid add-on is worth it.
- **Best platform:** LinkedIn, Facebook
- **Type:** evergreen how-to with a seasonal hook
- **Lead sources (opened 22 November 2026):**
  - [Microsoft Support: How Copilot Chat works with and without a Microsoft Copilot licence](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/microsoft-365-copilot/how-copilot-chat-works-with-and-without-a-microsoft-365-copilot-license)

- **Check in research:** which business plans include Copilot Chat, whether it's at no extra cost in the UK, and what it can't do without the add-on. No prices in posts unless verified and relevant.
