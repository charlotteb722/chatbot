## Summary

On 10 November 2026 Google started rolling out two Gemini features in Gmail, AI conversation summaries and "Help me write", to all Google Workspace users aged 18 and over. For a UK small business on any Google Workspace Business plan, both are available without an add-on, and the UK plans start at £7 per user per month. There's a catch for sole traders on a free @gmail.com address: summaries work in the UK, but Help me write is US-only for personal accounts. The practical point is that a business already paying for Workspace can start drafting enquiry replies with AI today, as long as someone checks every draft before it goes out.

## Assumptions and interpretation

These are my reading of the facts. Drafts may use them only as clearly framed opinion or explanation.

- **A1.** A UK small business on any Google Workspace Business plan can use Help me write and conversation summaries now, or within the 15-day rollout, at no extra cost. Based on F1, F5, F10 and F16.
- **A2.** A UK sole trader using a free @gmail.com address can get conversation summaries but not Help me write or Suggested Replies (F10). The announcement in F4 mentions personal accounts in general terms, but the Help Centre (F5, F10) is more specific and says US only. I'd go with the Help Centre and phrase this as "Google's help pages say..."
- **A3.** On the UK page, the higher price in each pair (£7, £14, £22) is the monthly flexible price, and the lower one (£5.90, £11.80, £18.40) is the price with a one-year commitment. The arithmetic fits the "save 16%" wording, but the page text I read doesn't label which is which.
- **A4.** The most useful small-business uses are replying to repeated enquiries (prices, availability, areas covered), catching up on long supplier or customer threads, and turning rough notes into a polite reply. These are examples, not Google claims.
- **A5.** The human check matters most for prices, dates and promises, because F9 says drafts can be inaccurate. This is the natural point to show expertise: "AI drafts it, you check it."
- **A6.** I have no verified figure for time saved. Posts should talk about "faster" or "less blank-page time", not hours or percentages.

## Suggested practical takeaway

Pick the one enquiry you answer most often. In Gmail, reply to a real example, click Help me write, and give it a specific prompt: who it's for, what they asked, your actual price or availability, and "friendly, British English, under 120 words" (F6, F7). Check every fact before sending (F9). Keep the version you're happy with somewhere you can reuse it.

## Things to check

1. **Personal accounts (A2).** If much of your audience are sole traders on free Gmail, the post needs to say clearly that Help me write isn't available to them in the UK yet. Otherwise the post will disappoint them.
2. **Prices (F15, A3).** I'd suggest the posts mention "from £7 per user per month" only if it adds something. The how-to works without prices.
3. **Rollout timing (F2).** The 15-day window ends around 25 November. If you publish after that, "rolling out now" should become "now available".
4. **Admin Help pages.** These couldn't be opened (403), so there are no verified admin step-by-step instructions. The posts should stick to "your admin can switch it off" (F3).
