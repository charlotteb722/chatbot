## Audience

UK sole traders and small business owners, in two groups:

- **Free Gmail users** (an @gmail.com address, no paid plan)
- **Paid Google Workspace users** (their own business domain on a Workspace Business plan)

Every post must make clear which group a feature is for. A post should only recommend a feature to a group if the facts below confirm that group has it in the UK.

## Summary

Google's Gemini features in Gmail are now useful to most UK small businesses, but what you get depends on your account. If you're on free Gmail in the UK, you can use AI summaries of long email threads and set reminders from them. The drafting tools (Help me write, Suggested Replies and Proofread) need a paid Google Workspace plan, or a paid Google AI plan on a personal account. Google is also extending summaries and Help me write to all Workspace users aged 18 and over, rolling out from 10 November 2026. So the practical message is simple: everyone can save time *reading* email, and Workspace users can also save time *writing* it, provided they check every draft before sending.

## Assumptions and interpretation

These are my reading of the facts. Drafts may use them only as clearly framed opinion or advice.

- **A1.** Any UK business on a Google Workspace Business plan can use Help me write, summaries, Suggested Replies and Proofread now. Based on F1, F5, F10 and F15. Some may still be inside the 15-day rollout window (F2).
- **A2.** UK free Gmail users get summaries and reminders but not the drafting tools. The announcement (F4) mentions personal accounts in general terms. The Help Centre (F5, F10) says Help me write and Suggested Replies are US-only for personal accounts. I've followed the Help Centre, and posts should say "Google's help pages say..." rather than state it absolutely.
- **A3.** A personal account with a paid Google AI plan (Plus, Pro or Ultra) also gets the drafting tools (F10). This is a possible third option for sole traders, but its UK availability and pricing weren't researched. I'd leave it out of posts, or mention it only as "or a paid Google AI plan" without detail.
- **A4.** The most useful time-savers are:
  - **Everyone:** summarising long customer or supplier threads before replying, and turning deadlines in emails into reminders.
  - **Workspace users:** drafting replies to repeated enquiries, softening or tightening a tricky reply, and checking tone before sending.
  These are examples, not Google claims.
- **A5.** A human check is essential for prices, dates and promises (F9). This is where the posts show expertise: "AI does the first draft, you make the decisions."
- **A6.** There's no verified figure for time saved, so posts must not quote hours or percentages.
- **A7.** Gemini output may use US spelling (unverified). Suggesting "write in British English" in the prompt is a reasonable tip, framed as advice, not a Google feature.

- Write for both groups. Where a feature is Workspace-only, say so in plain words ("if you use Google Workspace...").
- Quote Google's button labels exactly as they appear ("Summarize this email", "Help me write", "Remind me"), even though the spelling is American. Everything else is British English.

- Tone: friendly, professional and practical, following `charlotte-style`. Start from a real email problem, show the steps, and end with one thing to try.

## Suggested practical takeaways

**Free Gmail:** next time a long email thread lands with a customer or supplier, open it, click "Summarize this email" (F13), and check the key points before replying. If it mentions a deadline, click "Remind me" (F14).

**Google Workspace:** pick the enquiry you answer most often. Reply to a real example, click Help me write, and give it a specific prompt: who it's for, what they asked, your actual answer, and "friendly, British English, under 120 words" (F6, F7). Check every detail before sending (F9).
