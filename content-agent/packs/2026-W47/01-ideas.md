## 1. ChatGPT now lets businesses claim a profile. What UK businesses should do while they wait

- **What's happening:** On 10 November OpenAI launched "Businesses in ChatGPT" in beta. Businesses can claim and update the profile ChatGPT shows when it mentions them, and see how often they come up. According to coverage, it's limited to the US for now, with more markets planned.
- **Why it matters to a UK small business:** More customers are asking AI assistants for recommendations ("a good bookkeeper near Reading"). Even before UK access arrives, what ChatGPT says about you depends on what's on your website and public listings.
- **Angle:** A 20-minute check: ask ChatGPT what it knows about your business, then fix the gaps on your website and Google Business Profile. This ties straight into your website and digital support work.
- **Best platform:** LinkedIn, because it's a strategic, slightly forward-looking point that suits business owners there.
- **Type:** news plus how-to
- **Lead sources (search result only, not opened):**
  - [OpenAI: Introducing Businesses in ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/), 10 November 2026
  - [AI Business: OpenAI's Businesses in ChatGPT targets enterprise trust](https://aibusiness.com/generative-ai/openai-s-businesses-in-chatgpt-targets-enterprise-trust) (secondary)
- **Check in research:** whether the UK is included, and what "eligible" means.

## 2. Gemini in Gmail is reaching more Workspace users. Use it to answer customer enquiries faster

- **What's happening:** Google is expanding Gemini's "Help me write" and email summaries in Gmail to more Google Workspace users, rolling out from 10 November. Admins can switch it off.
- **Why it matters to a UK small business:** Plenty of small firms run on Google Workspace and spend real time on repetitive enquiry emails. If it's already in their Gmail, the cost is nothing extra.
- **Angle:** How to turn a recurring enquiry ("Do you cover my area? What do you charge?") into a reusable reply in about 20 minutes, and what to check before sending. Google also published four small-business examples on 19 November, but that's Google marketing itself, so treat it as illustration only.
- **Best platform:** Facebook and Instagram for the practical how-to, plus a strong carousel ("5 steps to faster enquiry replies").
- **Type:** how-to
- **Lead sources (search result only, not opened):**
  - [Google Workspace Updates: Expanding Gemini in Gmail to more Google Workspace users](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/expanding-gemini-in-gmail-to-more-Google-Workspace-users.html), 10 November 2026
  - [Google: Small businesses, big results](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/workspace/google-workspace-gemini-small-business-stories/), 19 November 2026 (vendor marketing)
- **Check in research:** which Workspace editions are included, the exact rollout timing, and any age or country limits.

## 3. A third of UK businesses now use AI. Where should the other two thirds start?

- **What's happening:** The government's AI Adoption Survey 2026 (DSIT, published 29 October) reportedly found 32% of UK businesses using at least one AI technology, up from 16% in 2025, with adoption higher among larger firms. Other 2026 surveys give very different figures (Simply Business 47%, Enterprise Nation 21% regular use) because they measure different things.
- **Why it matters to a UK small business:** It's reassurance that they're not behind, plus a sensible place to begin. The difference between the surveys is itself a useful, honest point.
- **Angle:** "You're not late. Here's a sensible first step", with three low-risk starter tasks. It also lets you show you read the evidence properly instead of quoting the most dramatic number.
- **Best platform:** LinkedIn
- **Type:** evergreen with a news hook
- **Note:** published 23 days ago, so just outside the 14-day window. I included it because it's the most authoritative UK figure available.
- **Lead sources (search result only, not opened):**
  - [GOV.UK (DSIT): AI Adoption Survey 2026, executive summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-adoption-survey-2026/ai-adoption-survey-report-2026-executive-summary), 29 October 2026
  - [Simply Business: Small business AI usage doubles](https://www.simplybusiness.co.uk/knowledge/business-news/small-business-ai-usage-doubles/), 2026 (secondary survey)
- **Check in research:** the figures for micro and small businesses specifically, top uses and barriers, and the survey method.

## 4. Is Microsoft 365 Copilot worth paying for in a small business? A plain-English check

- **What's happening:** Microsoft's Copilot Business plan for firms under 300 users launched in December 2025. Partner pages mention a 25% promotional offer running to 31 December 2026, and bundled plan pricing.
- **Why it matters to a UK small business:** Many small firms already pay for Microsoft 365 and are being offered Copilot. A year-end offer creates pressure to decide quickly.
- **Angle:** A simple "worth it or not" test: list three weekly tasks, estimate the time saved, and compare that with the monthly cost per person. You use M365 yourself, so this is credible.
- **Best platform:** LinkedIn
- **Type:** news plus decision guide
- **Time-sensitive:** if the offer is real, it ends 31 December.
- **Lead sources (search result only, not opened):**
  - [Microsoft Tech Community: Introducing Microsoft 365 Copilot Business](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft365copilotblog/introducing-microsoft-365-copilot-business-empowering-small-and-medium-businesse/4469700), November 2025 (more than 12 months old, so it needs rechecking)
  - [Microsoft Partner blog: partner-led SMB Microsoft 365 Copilot](https://partner.microsoft.com/en-ca/blog/article/partner-led-smb-m365-copilot) (partner channel. Not on the network allowlist yet.)
- **Check in research:** current UK pricing in £ from microsoft.com/en-gb, and whether the offer applies in the UK. All the figures so far are US dollars.

## 5. AI is making scam emails more convincing. Five checks for a small team

- **What's happening:** The ICO published advice in May on protecting organisations from AI-powered cyber threats, and AI-written phishing and voice scams keep growing. This isn't this week's news, but it's always relevant.
- **Why it matters to a UK small business:** Small firms are frequent targets and rarely have IT support. One fake invoice or "urgent payment" request can cost more than any AI tool saves.
- **Angle:** Five practical checks anyone in a small team can follow, such as verifying payment changes by phone and using a family-style "safe word" for urgent requests. It shows you care about using AI safely, not just selling it.
- **Best platform:** Instagram carousel, with a Facebook post
- **Type:** evergreen
- **Lead sources (search result only, not opened):**
  - ICO: "Five steps to protect your organisation from AI-powered cyber threats", reported 13 May 2026. The exact URL wasn't found; the news index is at [ico.org.uk news and blogs](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/)
  - National Cyber Security Centre small business guidance would be the natural second source. It isn't on the allowlist yet.
- **Check in research:** the ICO page itself, and NCSC guidance (needs `www.ncsc.gov.uk` added to the network).
