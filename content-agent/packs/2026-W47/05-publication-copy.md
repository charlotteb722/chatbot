If you're wondering where AI could help in your business, your inbox is a good place to start.

Repetitive email makes a sensible first test. It happens every week, you already know what a good reply looks like, and you can check the result before anything is sent. That makes it a low-risk way to try AI on real work before committing to anything bigger.

Gemini in Gmail is an easy way in, but what you get depends on your account.

## Blog article

**Title:** Gemini in Gmail for UK small businesses: what you can use now

**Meta description:** Which Gemini features in Gmail UK sole traders and small businesses can use on free Gmail or Google Workspace, with simple steps to save time on email.

For many small businesses, it's not the hard emails that eat the time. It's the long threads you reread before replying, and the same questions you answer over and over.

Gemini in Gmail can help with both, but what you can use depends on your account. Here's a plain guide to what works in the UK, based on Google's own help pages, checked on 21 and 22 November 2026.

## First, which account do you have?

There are two common set-ups:

- **Free Gmail:** a personal address ending in @gmail.com, with no paid plan.
- **Google Workspace:** a paid plan, usually with your own business email address.

Google lists Gemini features differently for each, so check which you use first.

## What free Gmail and Workspace users can both use: summaries

Google's help pages say AI summaries of email threads are available on both personal and work accounts, including free Gmail in the UK. There are two conditions: you need a supported language such as English, and smart features need to be switched on. On Workspace, your admin can also switch Gemini features off. It then shows the key points and replies at the top of a long thread.

To use it on a computer:

1. Open Gmail and open the thread.
2. At the top of the thread, click "Summarize this email".
3. Read the overview before you scroll back through the messages.

If the thread includes a task with a due date, Google says the summary can suggest a reminder. If you see "Remind me", click it and the task appears in Google Tasks and Calendar and at the top of your inbox when it's due. Don't expect it on every thread.

If you can't see the summary button, check your Gmail settings. Google names two settings to switch on: "Smart features in Gmail, Chat, and Meet" and "Smart features in Google Workspace". Turning them off also turns off other AI tools, such as writing help.

## What Google Workspace users can also use: Help me write

If you use Google Workspace, you can also use Help me write to draft a new email or to improve one you've started. Google is rolling out Help me write and summaries to all Workspace users aged 18 and over, starting on 10 November 2026, and says it may take up to 15 days for the features to appear.

To draft a reply on a computer:

1. Open the email and click Reply.
2. Type what you want in the prompt bar at the bottom of the reply. If you can't see it, click Help me write.
3. Click Create, then refine the draft with a new instruction or with the Formalize, Friendly or Shorten options.

Google's advice is to be specific: say who you're writing to, what the email is about, what needs to happen, and the tone you want.

Here's an illustration. It's a made-up example, not a real business. A dog groomer gets the same question every week: "Do you have Saturday availability, and how much for a large dog?" They reply and ask Help me write: "Friendly reply to a new customer. We have Saturday slots from 9am. Large dogs take about two hours. Ask them to call to book. British English, under 120 words." Then they check the draft, fix anything that's off, and keep it to reuse.

Asking for British English is my suggestion, not a Google feature.

## What free Gmail users can't use in the UK (at the time of writing)

At the time of writing, Google's help documentation says Help me write isn't available to free personal Gmail accounts in the UK. It's listed as US only for personal accounts. The same applies to Suggested Replies. This reflects Google's published information today and may change.

## Before you rely on it

Google is clear that Gemini "may suggest inaccurate or inappropriate information" and shouldn't be relied on for legal, financial or other professional advice. Read every summary and draft. Check names, dates, prices and anything you're promising a customer. The AI does the first draft. You make the decisions.

If you'd rather it didn't run at all, you can switch these features off yourself, and Workspace admins can disable them for the whole business.

Pick one long email thread this week and try "Summarize this email" on it. If you use Google Workspace, take your most common customer question and draft one reply with Help me write. Keep the version you're happy with.
