## Summary

People are increasingly using AI to write subject access requests (SARs), the requests people make for a copy of the personal information an organisation holds about them. On 11 November 2026 the ICO published guidance confirming that an AI-written request is just as valid as any other. The normal one-month deadline applies, and a request isn't "complex" simply because it's long or confusing. Small businesses can ask for clarification when it's genuinely needed, only have to make a reasonable and proportionate search, and should watch for fake ID or letters of authority, which AI can also produce. Requests can arrive in any form, including verbally or on social media.

## Assumptions and interpretation

- **A1.** Most small businesses rarely get SARs, so a long AI-written one can feel alarming. A calm, simple process is the useful message.
- **A2.** A five-step routine (recognise it, note the date, check identity if needed, clarify only if necessary, search proportionately and reply securely) is a fair summary of F2-F8 and F12-F15. It's advice, not ICO wording.
- **A3.** The content isn't legal advice. Point readers to the ICO guidance for detail.
