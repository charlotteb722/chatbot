# How small businesses can use AI to manage customer enquiries

*Draft for Charlotte's review*

If you run a small business, you probably answer the same handful of questions every week. What are your opening hours? Do you deliver to my area? How much does it cost? Can I change my booking?

None of these questions is difficult. The trouble is that they arrive at awkward times, through different channels, and each one takes a few minutes to answer well. Those minutes add up, and the enquiries that really need your attention can get buried under the routine ones.

AI tools can help with this. Not by replacing you, and not by talking to your customers unsupervised, but by taking the first draft off your hands so you can spend your time checking and sending rather than writing from scratch.

## Start with the enquiries you already get

Before choosing any tool, spend a week noting down the enquiries that come in. You don't need a spreadsheet for this. A note on your phone is fine. For each one, jot down what the person asked and roughly how long it took you to reply.

By the end of the week you'll usually see a pattern. Most small businesses find that a small number of question types make up the bulk of their enquiries. Those are the ones worth working on first.

## Write your answers down once

This is the step people skip, and it's the one that makes everything else work. AI can only give good answers if it has good information to work from.

Take your most common questions and write a short, accurate answer to each. Include the details a customer would need: prices, timings, what's included, what isn't, and what they should do next. Keep it in a single document you can update when things change.

Even if you never use AI, this document is useful. It means you, or anyone helping you, can answer consistently.

## Use AI to draft replies

Once you have your answers written down, you can paste them into a general AI assistant such as ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot along with a customer's message, and ask it to draft a reply.

A simple prompt might look like this:

> Here is information about my business: [paste your answers document]. A customer has sent this message: [paste the message]. Draft a friendly, short reply using only the information above. If the information doesn't cover their question, say so and suggest I reply personally.

That last instruction matters. AI tools can sound confident even when they're guessing, so telling it to flag gaps is a simple way to stop it inventing a price or a policy you don't have.

Read every draft before you send it. You'll often make small changes to the tone or add a personal detail, and that's the point. You're still the one replying. You've just saved yourself the blank page.

## Sort enquiries so the important ones don't wait

If you get a lot of messages, AI can also help you sort them. You can paste in a batch of enquiries and ask it to group them, for example into booking changes, pricing questions, complaints and new business. That makes it easier to see what needs a quick answer and what needs proper thought.

Complaints and anything involving money, refunds or personal circumstances should always get your direct attention. AI can help you draft a careful response, but the judgement is yours.

## When to consider something more automated

Some businesses eventually want enquiries answered automatically, for example through a chat box on their website or an automated email reply. These tools exist, and some are built into platforms you may already use for bookings, email or social media.

They're worth considering once you've done the steps above, because they rely on the same thing: clear, accurate answers you've already written. If your information is out of date or incomplete, an automated tool will spread that mistake to every customer who asks.

Before switching anything on, check a few things:

- What happens when the tool doesn't know the answer? There should be an easy way for the customer to reach a person.
- Where does customer information go, and is that acceptable under data protection rules? If you're unsure, check the provider's privacy terms.
- Is it clear to customers that they're talking to an automated tool?
- Who will review the conversations, and how often?

## Keep it small and keep it checked

You don't need to change everything at once. A sensible first goal is to have your common answers written down and to use AI to draft replies to just one type of enquiry. If that saves you time and the replies are good, add another.

The aim isn't to remove yourself from customer conversations. It's to spend less time typing the same thing and more time on the enquiries where your knowledge and care actually make a difference.
